# Introduction to Lodestone
*What it is, how it thinks, and how the pieces fit together*

---

## What Is Lodestone?

Lodestone is a product management platform designed to help product managers turn messy, fragmented feedback into clear, coherent plans — and share those plans through strong narratives and presentations.

At its core, Lodestone helps PMs:

- Capture product ideas from real conversations
- Shape those ideas into structured plans
- Communicate WHAT is being built, WHY it matters, and WHEN it is coming
- Achieve organizational alignment through storytelling

Lodestone is built first and foremost for the **individual Product Manager**, while steadily evolving to support **Product Leaders managing teams of PMs**.

---

## What Lodestone Is Not

Lodestone is intentionally focused. It is not:

- **A developer ticketing system** — it doesn't replace JIRA, Asana, or Azure DevOps
- **A project management system** — it doesn't replace MS Project, Monday.com, or task-based execution tools
- **A workflow enforcer** — it doesn't mandate rigid processes or linear paths

Lodestone sits between customer reality and delivery execution. It focuses exclusively on the product management layer — capturing intent, building plans, and communicating them clearly.

---

## The Core Belief

Lodestone is built on a simple but strong belief:

> Product managers do their best work when they spend more time sharing and refining plans with stakeholders, not assembling artifacts.

A user knows Lodestone is working for them when:
- They are creating and sharing plans more frequently
- They are presenting roadmaps and strategies more often
- They are spending less time formatting and more time aligning people

---

## The Planning Stack

Lodestone is organized around core objects that reflect how product work actually unfolds in the real world:

| Object | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Extracts** | Raw ideas surfaced from meeting transcripts and competitive reports using AI |
| **Features** | Concrete initiatives a product team may pursue |
| **Documents** | Written artifacts that explain and justify Features |
| **Roadmaps** | Narrative plans that explain WHAT is being built and WHY |
| **Releases** | Progress views that communicate WHEN things are happening |
| **Strategy** | Long-term investment decisions that force tradeoffs over time |
| **Competitive Intelligence** | AI-generated competitor tracking and market analysis |
| **Financial Forecast** | Financial modeling (NPV, ROI, IRR) for individual Features |

This stack is **not prescriptive**. Users may start at any point depending on their needs. A PM might begin by uploading a customer call transcript, or they might start by sketching out a roadmap. Lodestone accommodates both.

---

## WHAT, WHY, and WHEN

One of Lodestone's most important mental models is the separation of planning concerns. Different modules answer different questions:

- **Roadmaps** answer: WHAT are we building, and WHY does it matter?
- **Releases** answer: WHEN is it happening, and how is progress tracking?
- **Strategy** answers: Where are we intentionally investing over time, and what tradeoffs are we making?

Keeping these questions separate prevents overloaded artifacts and keeps communication clear. A roadmap that tries to also track delivery status becomes harder to share. A release board that tries to explain strategic rationale becomes harder to read.

---

## Objects Over Workflows

Lodestone is intentionally **object-centric**, not workflow-centric.

Product teams don't follow linear paths. Planning is iterative. Different people enter the system at different stages. Rather than enforcing a single "right" flow, Lodestone lets:

- Features exist independently of any roadmap or release
- Objects relate to each other flexibly
- Structure emerge through usage, not prescription

This means you're never forced to complete one step before starting another. The platform adapts to how you actually work.

---

## AI as an Assistant, Not an Author

AI is woven throughout Lodestone — in Extracts, Documents, Roadmaps, Strategy exports, and more. It exists to:

- Eliminate blank-page work
- Speed up iteration
- Surface insights from raw data

AI does **not** replace human judgment. All AI-generated content in Lodestone is editable, clearly marked, and designed as a starting point — not a finished product. You stay in control of everything the platform produces.

---

## Key Design Principles

A few principles shape how Lodestone behaves across every module:

**Guidance over enforcement** — Lodestone provides strong defaults and opinionated visualizations, but rarely forces a single way of doing things. You can adapt it to your context.

**Narration over execution** — Execution lives in delivery tools. Lodestone exists to explain and align. Its outputs are presentations, exports, and shared artifacts — not tickets or task lists.

**Scarcity as a forcing function** — Where Lodestone does enforce constraints (like exactly three Strategy dimensions), it does so intentionally. Constraints force meaningful choices. When everything is a priority, nothing is.

## How the Module Docs Are Organized

Each Lodestone module has four documentation pages:

- **Overview & Concepts** — what the module is, why you'd use it, key terminology
- **How-tos & Workflows** — step-by-step guides for the main user journeys
- **Advanced Features & Customization** — deeper configuration, integrations, and power-user options
- **Troubleshooting & FAQs** — common problems, edge cases, and answers to frequent questions

If you're new to Lodestone, start with the Overview & Concepts page for whichever module you're working with. If you already know what you need to do, go straight to How-tos & Workflows.

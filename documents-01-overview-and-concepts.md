# Documents — Overview & Concepts
*What they are, why they matter, and how they fit into Lodestone*

---

## What Is a Document?

A Document in Lodestone is a written artifact created to provide context, clarity, and narrative support for a Feature. Documents reflect the real-world artifacts product managers are expected to produce — justifications, requirements, communication drafts, go-to-market assets — without requiring you to start from a blank page.

Every Document in Lodestone is tied to a Feature. Documents don't exist independently; they exist to explain and support the Feature they belong to.

---

## Why Documents Exist

Across a single Feature, a PM might be expected to produce a business justification, a set of requirements, acceptance criteria, a customer-facing announcement, and enablement materials for the sales team. Individually, each feels manageable. Collectively, they consume days or weeks of effort — most of it formatting and structuring rather than thinking.

Lodestone uses AI to generate a strong first draft instantly, so you can spend your time iterating on ideas rather than assembling documents. The goal is to shift effort from blank-page anxiety to meaningful refinement.

> **Key idea:** AI-generated Documents are starting points, not finished products. All content is fully editable, and you retain full authorship. Lodestone deliberately avoids black-box generation — you always see and control what gets produced.

---

## Documents as First-Class Objects

Documents are first-class objects in Lodestone, meaning they can be exported, shared, and used independently of the rest of the platform. This matters because:

- Different teams use different subsets of documents — a team focused on execution needs user stories and UAT plans; a team focused on alignment needs opportunity canvases and PRDs
- Documents are reused across different contexts and audiences
- Each artifact has standalone value outside of Lodestone

This design means you're never forced into producing all document types for every Feature — you use what's useful for your context.

---

## Available Document Types

Lodestone currently supports eleven document types, organized by their primary job:

### Thinking aids
These help you clarify reasoning, articulate tradeoffs, and build a shared understanding of what you're building and why.

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Opportunity Canvas | A structured one-pager capturing the problem, solution, value, and risks for a Feature |
| Product Requirements Document (PRD) | A detailed description of what needs to be built and why |

### Delivery artifacts
These support execution and handoff to engineering, QA, and other teams.

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| User Stories | Bite-sized descriptions of functionality from the user's perspective |
| UAT Test Plan | Acceptance criteria and test scenarios for validating the Feature |
| User Guide Documentation | End-user documentation explaining how to use the Feature |
| Prototype Prompt | A prompt designed to be pasted directly into an AI prototyping tool |

### Communication assets
These support go-to-market, stakeholder communication, and external announcements.

| Document | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Customer Email | A direct communication to customers about the Feature |
| Company Blog Post | A long-form external post announcing or explaining the Feature |
| Twitter Posts | Short-form social content for X/Twitter |
| Instagram Posts | Visual-first social content for Instagram |
| LinkedIn Posts | Professional social content for LinkedIn |

---

## Where Documents Live

Documents live on the Feature Detail page, which you access by clicking a Feature name in the backlog. From there you can generate, edit, export, and revisit any Document associated with that Feature.

Documents are living artifacts — they're meant to be updated as a Feature evolves, not written once and forgotten. That said, if you choose to regenerate a Document with AI, be aware that regeneration immediately overwrites the existing content with no warning and no recovery path. If you've made edits worth keeping, copy them before regenerating.

---

## How Documents Relate to Features

- A Document cannot exist without a Feature
- A Feature can have one Document per type — one Opportunity Canvas, one PRD, one Customer Email, and so on
- Deleting a Feature also deletes all of its associated Documents

---

## Exporting Documents

All Documents can be exported as PDFs. The Opportunity Canvas has an additional export option — it can be exported as a single PowerPoint slide. Opportunity Canvases also appear automatically as an appendix when you export a Roadmap as a PPT presentation.

Exports are designed to be immediately shareable and presentation-ready, without requiring any additional formatting work.
